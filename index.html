How it works

What it measures

Words, phrases and sentence habits that AI models use far more than human fiction writers ("a testament to", "the air was thick with", "a flicker of…", not-x-but-y). Each word and phrase is weighted by how much more often AI fiction uses it than human fiction. Only prose is scored: W++/PList blocks, key-value lists and pasted instructions are set aside.

The score

60% AI-favored words, 25% AI-favored phrases, 15% sentence structures, plus up to 10 points for heavy em-dash use and 40 per obvious chatbot leftover ("Certainly! Here is…", "I hope this helps!"). Each part is scaled so 100 means "as AI-styled as the most AI-styled tenth of AI-written character cards". The ⓘ next to a result says where it sits among real human and AI-written cards (with how many cards that comparison uses).

Bands: Original-ish under 40, Mixed 40–60, Common 60+ (how common the card's phrasing is in AI writing). Each has a just-for-fun tagline: meatbag adjacent, mixed signals, might just be slop. They're set to keep false positives down. Cards from 2023 (108,129: Korshuk, JanitorAI, Chub; mostly human, though some AI-written cards from that year are in there) split 94 / 4 / 2% across the three bands, median 12. AI-written cards (2,855) split 43 / 28 / 29%, median 45, so a low score doesn't clear anyone either. Separation (AUC): 0.86; at a 2% false-positive rate on those 2023 cards it flags 29% of AI cards. Numbers from the BBBScorer v0.8.1 run.

Description and first message: scored separately, then blended by length (each counts up to 400 words), so a long description can't hide the greeting. When both have 150+ words of prose and land 25+ points apart, the card is flagged as mixed, for example a hand-written description with an AI-written greeting.

Not an AI detector

It measures a house style, not authorship. A human who writes in that style scores high (pulp fantasy and romance share a lot of it), and a model told to avoid it can score low. It also says nothing about whether a card is good.

Short and compact cards

W++, PLists and lists are set aside, so a compact card's score mostly rests on its greeting, and a short card's on a few hundred words. Few words means few chances to hit anything: AI-written cards under 250 words of prose usually land in Original-ish, so treat a low score on a bite-sized card as "not enough to go on", not as a clean bill.

Read-outs (unscored)

Marked Unscored on the result: shown to help, never counted in the score. Experimental marks ones still being tuned. They are: shared clichés humans and models both use, RP slop (TheDrummer's UnslopNemo list of RP chat clichés), human tells (typos and slips models rarely make), AI-favorite names, the pulp read (how much of the score is genre vocabulary), repeated wording, paragraph evenness, the old-school AI read and the sexual-content read (both below).

Old-school AI (experimental)

The essay and character-sheet register of older models: ChatGPT-era GPT-3.5 / GPT-4 and 2024-era open models such as Phi-4, Mistral Small, Gemma 3 and Llama 3 ("a testament to", "meticulously", "a force to be reckoned with", "her sharp wit"). The word and phrase lists the score uses were built from fiction, so they miss this register. Newer models almost never write this way, so it points at older bots, and people use some of it too. It counts different items, not repeats, and items from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" list that turned out as common in 2023 cards as in any model are shown greyed and don't count.

Sexual content

Whether the card has sexual content (explicit or suggestive wording) and NSFW-type tags, read from every field and greeting, whatever is ticked for scoring. It's a word list: it can miss content that is only implied, and it skips the usual false alarms ("Sex: Female", "sexual orientation", "Dick" as a name, "no NSFW" rules).

The leaderboard

Models: character cards each model wrote for us from the same requests: the name, creator note and tags of real 2023 human cards, asked for in seven request styles: the five formats (free prose, formatted prose, lists, compact, and in voice as both first person and interview) plus a lazy prompt that leaves the format to the model ("make me a character bot for X"). Only those plain requests are ranked; the report for each model also shows cards written with "don't sound like AI" instructions and rewrites of human cards. Bars show the middle half of each model's cards, the line marks the median. Under each model: the range of its medians across request styles, and its lazy-prompt median. Small samples (under 20 cards) are marked with an asterisk; tick Hide models with under 20 cards to leave them out. Sort by BBBScorer, the original slop score, or # cards.

References: real card sets scored with the same version: early 2023 human cards (Korshuk, JanitorAI, Chub), later Chub and Perchance cards (a mix of human and AI-assisted writing), and AI-written persona sets. Dates are on the line under each set's name.

Just for fun (not ranked): UnslopNemo and its base Mistral Nemo (UnslopNemo was tuned to strip stock phrasing from RP chat replies, not to write cards); Phi-4, a small general model nobody writes cards with; extra card sets of unknown origin (Perchance NSFW cards, Someone's SillyTavern Archive); and failed models, on a hatched red background: most of their cards failed the format checks (MythoMax ignores the format, Hermes 3 loops until it's cut off, about 40% of Aion RP's cards fail), so the usable rest isn't a fair sample.

Pulp read (experimental)

Pulpy fantasy, horror and romance writing shares a lot of vocabulary with AI models ("ancient", "shadows", "crimson"). The pulp read shows how much of a card's AI-favored word weight is genre vocabulary that human genre cards use about as often, and what the score would be without it. A card can read as pulp rather than AI-styled, or as both. Unscored.

Format, genre and archetype

Every card gets a description format: free prose, formatted prose, lists, compact, in voice, structured + prose, boilerplate + prose, or boilerplate. The ⓘ next to it lists them all. Format isn't scored. The greeting is described by layout, point of view and tense, and genre (experimental) is a rough read from keywords and tags; hover any dotted-underlined word on the result for what it means. Very long and very short cards get a size badge. (The just-for-fun archetype label is hidden while it's being redone.) Every tag and label is listed in BOTSTYLE.md.

Texture (experimental)

AI text tends to keep paragraphs close to the same length. The report shows that evenness and a likelihood combining it with the score. Unscored: careful human writers can be even too.

Your cards and privacy

Cards are scored in your browser. "Your cards" is stored in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you press "Share to public list" (when that list is switched on), which sends the card's name, link, score, version and what you tick. The public list never receives or keeps card text.

Versions

Every score carries the BBBScorer version, so results can be compared after the word lists or calibration change. Details, every tag and label, data and code: BOTSTYLE.md.
